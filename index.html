If this is your first time

New to AA?

Alcoholics Anonymous is a fellowship of people who share their experience, strength, and hope with each other so they can solve their common problem and help others recover from alcoholism. The only requirement for membership is a desire to stop drinking.

There are no dues or fees. Meetings are run entirely by members, with no professional staff, and nothing is expected of you beyond showing up.

  • Open meetings Anyone is welcome, including family, friends, or anyone curious about AA.
  • Closed meetings For those who have a drinking problem, or think they might.
  • Anonymity Who you see and what you hear stays in the room. First names only.

What to expect

Meeting formats explained

Early Birds: 6:00 AM, daily

A short, quiet meeting to start the day. Usually discussion-based, with members sharing briefly on a topic or reading. Open to anyone.

12x12: Noon, daily

A study meeting that works through the Twelve Steps and Twelve Traditions, one at a time, over the course of each cycle. For those working the program.

Night Meeting: 8:00 PM, daily

The main evening meeting. Format varies by night (discussion, speaker, or Big Book study). For those working the program.

Men's Group: 9:00 PM, Thursdays

A meeting for men to discuss topics specific to their experience in recovery.

Home Group Meeting: Noon, 1st Saturday of the month

A business meeting for Home Group members only. This is where the group makes decisions together and assigns service action items, not a recovery-discussion meeting.

Common questions

FAQ

Do I have to talk?

No. You're welcome to just listen for as many meetings as you'd like. Many people attend several times before saying anything at all.

I'm not sure I have a drinking problem. Can I still come?

Yes. The only requirement for membership is a desire to stop drinking. You don't need to be certain, and you don't need to have "hit bottom" in any particular way. Open meetings are especially suited for this; closed meetings are for anyone who has, or thinks they might have, a drinking problem.

Is what's said in a meeting actually confidential?

Anonymity is AA's core Tradition. Who attends and what's said in the room isn't repeated outside it. Using first names only is standard practice, in person and online.

What's a sponsor, and do I need one?

A sponsor is another AA member, further along in recovery, who guides someone newer through the Steps on a one-to-one basis. It's not required to attend meetings, but most members find one helpful. You can ask about finding a sponsor at any meeting.

What if I relapse?

Come back. Relapse doesn't disqualify anyone from AA, and many long-term members had setbacks along the way. Meetings remain open regardless.

Does it cost anything?

No dues or fees. Most meetings pass a basket for voluntary donations to cover rent and coffee, but contributing is never required.

Can I attend if I'm just worried about someone else's drinking?

Open meetings welcome family and friends. If it's specifically a loved one's drinking you want support around, Al-Anon (a separate, related fellowship) may be an even better fit. See the resources below.
